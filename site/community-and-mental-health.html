Healthcare settings

Half-hourly electricity in community hospitals and mental health sites

Inpatient units run around the clock, while the clinics, therapy spaces and offices beside them keep working hours. Separating the two is where the savings show.

By Paul Johnston, Managing Director, Kinetic Energy Consulting · Last updated 22 November 2026

Inpatient wards give a steady base with a daytime rise. Community clinics, therapy buildings and offices on the same estate should fall to a low base in the evening and at weekends. Many of these sites are older, gas-heated and spread across several buildings, so they are often good early candidates for heat pumps and solar.

What to check first

  • Clinics and therapy buildings heated and ventilated on the inpatient unit's 24-hour schedule
  • Weekend use in buildings that are closed at weekends
  • Kitchens, laundries and plant rooms with equipment left running
  • Base load steps after refurbishments
  • Headroom on smaller supplies before adding heat pumps or EV charging

Setting the hours in the analyser

Use the 24-7 preset for inpatient units and the clinic preset for outpatient and therapy buildings, then adjust the hours to match each site. Add bank holiday and Christmas closures for clinics.

Each site is compared with its own calendar, so getting the hours right matters more than the type of building.

Heat, capacity and solar

Smaller, separate supplies often have little spare capacity, so a heat pump for one building can need a connection upgrade. They also suit solar well where the inpatient unit is on the same supply. See heat decarbonisation.

The analyser's Solar tab shows how much of a system's output each supply would use, and passes it to the solar calculator, so the payback is based on your own profile. See sizing solar from half-hourly data.

How does your site compare?

To see whether a site's total use is high for its type, compare its kWh per square metre with similar sites. In England, ERIC site data is public; see estates returns and benchmarking. Then use the half-hourly pattern to find out why. Questions about the analyser are answered in the FAQ.

Questions people ask

Should we treat a community hospital as 24/7?

If it has inpatient beds, yes for that supply. Give separately metered clinics and offices their own opening hours.

Which sites should we analyse first?

Load every meter at once. The analyser ranks sites, so you can start where the evidence is strongest.
