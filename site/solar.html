Solar

Size hospital solar from how the site really runs

A 24-hour site uses most of what solar generates. Your half-hourly data shows exactly how much, at every system size.

By Paul Johnston, Managing Director, Kinetic Energy Consulting · Last updated 22 November 2026

On a hospital, solar is worth most when it's sized to the roof, car parks and connection you have, because a 24-hour site uses nearly all of a well-sized system's output. Your half-hourly data measures that share at each system size, replacing the guess that most estimates start from. The analyser passes the measured figure to the solar calculator, which works out what fits, what it costs and how soon it pays back.

Why healthcare sites fit solar

  • Demand is there when the sun is. Wards, theatres, imaging, kitchens and ventilation all run through the middle of the day, every day of the week. That's rare among building types, and it's what makes solar pay on hospitals.
  • Every unit used on site replaces a unit bought at the full import price.1 Export earns far less: under the Smart Export Guarantee, a supplier pays for exported power from systems up to 5 MW, at a rate the supplier sets.2
  • Rooftops rarely need planning permission in England, though listed buildings, conservation areas and car park canopies need more care, and almost every system needs DNO approval under G99.3
  • On-site renewable plant is exempt from business rates in England from April 2022, which matters because NHS hospitals pay rates.4
  • Funding has been aimed at NHS solar. Great British Energy has funded solar on NHS sites, and power purchase agreements need no capital at all.5

The figure that matters most: the share you use yourself

At a 25p unit price, each kWh of solar used on site saves 25p, while each kWh exported earns about 5p, the rate the calculator assumes.1 That five-to-one gap means the share used on site moves the result more than almost any other input.

The calculator starts at 80% if you don't measure it.1 For a 24-hour hospital that's often too cautious at the sizes most sites can fit, and too generous for very large arrays. For a health centre that closes at weekends, it can be too generous at almost any size. Half-hourly data settles it.

From your data to a solar estimate

  1. Load a year of half-hourly data for each main meter into the analyser. It runs in your browser. Need the files? See getting half-hourly data.
  2. Open the site's Solar tab. Add the postcode to set the sunshine for your area. The tab shows output, share used on site and share of the site's electricity at each size.
  3. Choose "Size solar for this site". The calculator opens with your figures. Draw the roofs and car parks on the map, and it sets the share used on site for the system that fits, then works out cost, savings and payback.

Only the annual use, the share used on site at a dozen sizes and the postcode, if entered, are passed, after the # in the web address. See the privacy notice.

What the solar calculator does

The solar calculator on this site uses the same method as the UK Solar Calculator, built by the same team.

  • Measures roofs and car parks from satellite imagery or dimensions you enter, for pitched and flat roofs, ground mounts and carports.1
  • Estimates generation at your postcode from the SAP 10.3 regional sunshine data.1,7
  • Prices the system from UK benchmarks per kWp, including an inverter replacement in year 13.1
  • Compares buying with a PPA over 25 years, and models a battery if you want one.1

What this can and cannot tell you

The analyser's estimate uses a typical UK solar day scaled to your region and the UK's seasonal pattern. It doesn't know about cloudy weeks, shading from plant or neighbouring blocks, roof orientation, or changes you'll make, such as removing a CHP engine. Treat it as a planning figure. The calculator's results are estimates, not a quotation.1,3

Questions people ask

Will a hospital use all the solar it generates?

Usually most of it, because wards, ventilation, imaging and fridges run through every sunny afternoon and at weekends. In our made-up community hospital, a system of up to about 300 kWp is used almost entirely on site. Very large systems still export at weekends and on long summer evenings, so measure it from your own data.

Is the 80% default in the solar calculator right for a hospital?

Often it's too low for a well-sized system on a 24-hour site and too high for a very large one. Load a year of half-hourly data into the analyser and pass the measured figure across instead.

Should a health centre or clinic be sized differently?

Yes. A building that closes at 18:30 and at weekends exports much more of its summer output, so the share used on site falls faster as the system gets bigger. Size it from that building's own meter, not the main hospital's.

Does solar cut our capacity charges?

No. Capacity charges are set per kVA of agreed capacity and standing charges per day, so they don't change. Solar cuts the units you import. It also does little for winter evening peaks, because it's dark by about 4pm in midwinter.

Is our meter data sent to the solar calculator?

No. Only the annual use, the share used on site at a dozen sizes and the postcode, if you entered one, are passed, in the part of the web address after the #. Browsers never send that part to a server.

Should we add a battery?

On a 24-hour hospital there is often little surplus to store unless the array is large. Check how much the site exports on sunny weekends in the analyser's estimate, then model the battery in the calculator.
