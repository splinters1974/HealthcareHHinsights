Half-hourly electricity in health centres and clinics

Health centres keep set hours, so use while they are closed is easy to see and often cheap to fix.

By Paul Johnston, Managing Director, Kinetic Energy Consulting · Last updated 23 November 2026

Expect a clear weekday pattern, falling to a low base load in the evening and at weekends, with extra hours on extended-access evenings and Saturdays. Many buildings have shared occupation, so the landlord, the practice and other services may each control part of the building.

What to check first

  • Heating, cooling and ventilation running all night or all weekend
  • Vaccine fridges and IT, which are part of the base load and should be steady
  • Extended hours that were never added to the building controls, or were never removed
  • Lighting in corridors, stairs and car parks on all night
  • Solar output that would be exported at weekends, before sizing a system

Setting the hours in the analyser

Start from the clinic preset (08:00 to 18:30 weekdays) and add any evening and Saturday sessions as open hours, so the out-of-hours figure only shows use that could be avoided.

Each site is compared with its own calendar, so getting the hours right matters more than the type of building.

Heat, capacity and solar

Health centres often have flat roofs and car parks that suit solar, but closed weekends mean a smaller share of output is used on site, so size from your data. See solar on healthcare estates.

The analyser's Solar tab shows how much of a system's output each supply would use, and passes it to the solar calculator, so the payback is based on your own profile. See sizing solar from half-hourly data.

How does your site compare?

To see whether a site's total use is high for its type, compare its kWh per square metre with similar sites. In England, ERIC site data is public; see estates returns and benchmarking. Then use the half-hourly pattern to find out why. Questions about the analyser are answered in the FAQ.

Questions people ask

Who can act on the findings in a shared building?

Whoever controls the plant, often the landlord or facilities provider. The analyser's PDF gives them the evidence in a form they can use.

Is a high base load normal for a health centre?

Some is: vaccine fridges, IT and security systems run all the time. A base load that rises without a clear reason is worth investigating.
