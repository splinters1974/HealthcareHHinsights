Glossary

Simple definitions of the terms used in half-hourly electricity data and on this site.

By Paul Johnston, Managing Director, Kinetic Energy Consulting · Last updated 18 November 2026

Half-hourly (HH) data is a record of the electricity a meter measures in every half hour: 48 readings a day, usually in kWh. The terms you will meet most often are kWh (energy, what you are billed for), kW (how fast electricity is being used at a given moment), base load (the demand that never switches off) and kVA (the unit your supply capacity is set in).

Agreed supply capacity
The maximum demand, in kVA, an electricity connection is set up for. It appears on larger electricity bills and is also called authorised or available capacity. More.
Annualised figure
A yearly figure scaled up from the valid readings available. It is used when the data covers less than a full year or has gaps.
Balancing Services Use of System (BSUoS)
A charge that covers the National Energy System Operator's cost of balancing the electricity system. A fixed rate per MWh, set in advance for six-month periods. More.
Base load
The level of demand a building rarely drops below: what never switches off. The analyser measures it as the 5th percentile of half-hourly demand. More.
Capacity Market (CM)
A scheme that pays providers to keep enough reliable capacity available. Businesses pay for it according to their electricity use between 4pm and 7pm on working days from November to February. More.
Climate Change Levy (CCL)
A tax on energy used by businesses, charged per kWh. Holders of a climate change agreement pay a reduced rate. More.
Closed-hours excess
Electricity used while a building should be closed, above its base load. The analyser ranks sites by it. More.
Coefficient of performance (COP)
How much heat a heat pump delivers for each unit of electricity it uses at a given moment. A COP of 3 means 3 kWh of heat for 1 kWh of electricity. The seasonal average is the SCOP.
Combined heat and power (CHP)
An engine, usually gas-fired, that generates electricity on site and recovers its waste heat for heating and hot water. Common on hospital estates; its carbon case has weakened as the grid has cleaned up. More.
Contracts for Difference (CfD)
Contracts that give low-carbon generators a fixed price. Suppliers pay a levy that rises when wholesale prices are low and falls, even to zero, when they are high. More.
Coverage
The share of expected half-hourly readings that are present and valid.
Degree days
A measure of how cold (heating degree days) or warm (cooling degree days) a period was. Used to compare energy use across periods with different weather.
Distribution network operator (DNO)
The company that owns and runs the local electricity network and agrees your supply capacity and any generation connection.
Distribution Use of System (DUoS)
Charges for using the local distribution network, set by your network operator. They combine unit rates that vary by time band, capacity charges and fixed daily charges. More.
DNO
Distribution network operator. The company that runs the local electricity network and your site's connection to it. The first two digits of your MPAN show which one. More.
EFPMS
The Estates and Facilities Performance Management System: the mandatory annual estates and facilities return for NHS Wales, including energy and CHP. More.
ERIC
The Estates Returns Information Collection: the mandatory annual estates and facilities return for NHS trusts in England, including energy use and costs. More.
G99
The engineering recommendation that sets how most generators, including larger solar systems and batteries, apply to the network operator to connect. More.
Green Plan
An NHS organisation's board-approved plan for reducing its carbon emissions and environmental impact, in line with the NHS net zero targets.
Half-hourly (HH) data
A record of the electricity used in every half hour: 48 readings a day.
Health Technical Memorandum (HTM)
Guidance on the design and operation of healthcare buildings and engineering services, such as HTM 03-01 (ventilation), HTM 04-01 (water safety) and HTM 06-01 (electrical services).
Heat decarbonisation plan (HDP)
A plan for replacing fossil fuel heating with low carbon alternatives, building by building, often required for public sector funding bids. More.
Heatmap
A chart of every half hour in a period, with one column per day and one row per half hour, coloured by demand. Also called a carpet plot. More.
kVA
Kilovolt-ampere. A measure of apparent power, and the unit supply capacity is set in. kVA = kW ÷ power factor.
kW
Kilowatt. A measure of power: the rate electricity is being used at a given moment. Divide a half-hourly reading in kWh by 0.5 to get the average kW for that half hour.
kWh
Kilowatt-hour. A measure of energy, and what you are billed for. Using 1 kW for one hour is 1 kWh.
Load factor
Average demand divided by peak demand. A low load factor means demand has short, high peaks.
Market-wide half-hourly settlement (MHHS)
The move to settle every electricity meter in Great Britain using half-hourly data. More.
MPAN
Meter point administration number. The 13-digit number on the bottom line of the supply number on an electricity bill. It identifies the supply point. More.
NHS Carbon Footprint
The emissions the NHS in England controls directly, including energy used in its buildings. The target is net zero by 2040. The Carbon Footprint Plus adds the supply chain, with a 2045 target. More.
NHS Net Zero Building Standard
The NHS England standard for new buildings and major upgrades that need HM Treasury business case approval, covering energy and whole-life carbon. More.
Non-energy costs
Everything in an electricity price apart from the wholesale energy: network charges, policy costs and taxes. More.
Nuclear RAB levy
A levy on suppliers that funds the building of new nuclear power stations under the Regulated Asset Base model, starting with Sizewell C. More.
Peak demand
The highest half-hourly average demand in a period, usually in kW or kVA.
Power factor
The ratio of real power (kW) to apparent power (kVA), between 0 and 1. Equipment such as motors lowers it.
Power purchase agreement (PPA)
A contract to buy electricity from a generator at an agreed price, often from a solar system a funder owns on your site. More.
Profile class
The first two digits of the top line of the supply number. 00 means the supply is settled half-hourly; 01 to 08 mean use is estimated from standard profiles. More.
Public Sector Decarbonisation Scheme (PSDS)
The UK government grant scheme, run by Salix, that funded heat and energy projects in English public buildings from 2020. No new funding is being committed beyond awarded projects. More.
Red, amber and green bands
The time bands most network operators use for distribution unit rates, with a different price in each. Each operator sets its own times. More.
Renewables Obligation (RO)
A scheme requiring suppliers to present certificates for each MWh they supply, or pay a buy-out price. The cost is passed on per unit. More.
Settlement
The process that works out how much electricity each supplier's customers used, and when, so suppliers pay the right amount for it.
Time-of-use tariff
A tariff with different unit rates at different times of day, such as day and night rates.
Transmission Network Use of System (TNUoS)
Charges for using the national high-voltage transmission network. For business sites, mainly a fixed charge per site per day, banded by agreed capacity or consumption. More.
Wholesale price
The price suppliers pay for electricity on the wholesale market, often called the commodity cost. More.
