Glossary

The estates, energy and data terms used on this site, in plain words.

By Paul Johnston, Managing Director, Kinetic Energy Consulting · Last updated 22 November 2026

These are the terms estates teams meet when they work with half-hourly electricity data and plan decarbonisation: estates terms such as ERIC, HTM and theatre setback; heat, solar and grid terms such as COP, G99 and firm headroom; data terms such as base load, kW and kVA; and the charges on the bill.

Healthcare estates

Authorised Person (Ventilation)
The person appointed in writing to manage specialised ventilation systems on a healthcare site, under HTM 03-01. Part of any project that changes theatre ventilation.
Capital departmental expenditure limit (CDEL)
The cap on capital spending set for NHS organisations. Whether a solar PPA or lease counts against it depends on how the contract is accounted for. More.
EFPMS
The Estates and Facilities Performance Management System: NHS Wales's mandatory annual estates return, covering energy and CHP among much else. More.
ERIC
The Estates Returns Information Collection: the mandatory annual estates and facilities return for NHS trusts in England, including energy use and cost by site. More.
Essential supplies
The parts of a hospital's electrical system backed by standby generators, so clinical services keep running if the grid supply fails.
Generator test
The routine on-load test of a hospital's standby generators, often monthly. While it runs the site imports less from the grid, which shows as a short dip in half-hourly data.
Green Plan
An NHS organisation's board-approved plan for cutting its carbon emissions and wider environmental impact, in line with the NHS net zero targets.
Health Technical Memorandum (HTM)
Guidance on designing and running healthcare buildings and engineering services. The ones that matter most for energy are HTM 03-01 (specialised ventilation), HTM 04-01 (water safety) and HTM 06-01 (electrical services). Scotland and Wales publish their own versions (SHTM, WHTM).
Heat decarbonisation plan (HDP)
A building-by-building plan for replacing fossil fuel heating with low carbon heat, often needed for public sector funding bids. More.
NHS Carbon Footprint
The emissions the NHS in England controls directly, including energy in its buildings, with a net zero target of 2040. The Carbon Footprint Plus adds the supply chain, with a 2045 target. More.
NHS Estates Technical Bulletin (NETB)
Technical guidance from NHS England that supplements the HTMs. NETB 2026/1 covers energy savings from controlling theatre ventilation. More.
NHS Net Zero Building Standard
The NHS England standard for new buildings and major refurbishments that need HM Treasury business case approval, covering operational energy and whole-life carbon. More.
PFI
Private Finance Initiative. On a PFI hospital a project company built the buildings and runs services under a long contract, and often holds the energy supply and meter data. Its consent is needed for works such as solar.
Public Sector Decarbonisation Scheme (PSDS)
The government grant scheme, run by Salix, that funded heat and energy projects in public buildings in England from 2020. New rounds are not being funded beyond 2027/28. More.
Theatre ventilation setback
Reducing or switching off operating theatre ventilation when the theatre is empty, then returning to full flow before use. One of the largest energy savings available on a hospital. More.
Ventilation Safety Group (VSG)
The multidisciplinary group in an NHS organisation that oversees specialised ventilation, including estates, infection prevention and clinical staff. The starting point for any change to theatre ventilation.
Water Safety Group
The group that owns an organisation's water safety plan for legionella and other risks under HTM 04-01. Changes to hot water temperatures or circulation need its agreement.

Heat, solar and the grid

Agreed supply capacity
The most a site's connection is contracted to draw, in kVA, shown on larger electricity bills. Also called authorised or available capacity. More.
Coefficient of performance (COP)
Heat delivered by a heat pump per unit of electricity used. A COP of 3 means 3 kWh of heat for 1 kWh of electricity. Averaged over a season it is the SCOP.
Combined heat and power (CHP)
A gas engine that generates electricity on site and uses its waste heat for heating and hot water. Common on hospitals; its carbon advantage over grid electricity has largely gone. More.
Distribution network operator (DNO)
The company that runs the local electricity network, agrees your supply capacity and approves generation such as solar. The first two digits of the MPAN show which one you are on. More.
Export limit
A cap, agreed with the DNO and enforced by a control system, on how much a site's solar or batteries can send back to the network. Usually costs a 24-hour hospital little. More.
Firm headroom
The largest constant extra load a supply could take in every half hour of the period without going over its agreed capacity less a safety margin. More.
G99
The engineering recommendation setting how larger generators, including most hospital solar systems and batteries, apply to the DNO to connect. More.
Power purchase agreement (PPA)
A contract to buy electricity from a generator at an agreed rate, often from a solar system a funder owns on your site. More.
Self-consumption
The share of a solar system's output used on site at the time it is generated rather than exported. On a 24-hour hospital it is usually high. More.

Reading half-hourly data

Annualised figure
A yearly total scaled up from the valid readings, used when the data falls short of a full year or has gaps. The analyser withholds it below six months.
Base load
The level a site's demand rarely falls below, measured by the analyser as the 5th percentile of half-hourly demand. On a hospital it is usually the largest part of the bill. More.
Closed-hours excess
Electricity used while a building should be closed, above its base load. Used for clinics, health centres and offices, not 24-hour sites. More.
Coverage
The share of expected half-hourly readings that are present and valid.
Degree days
A measure of how cold or warm a period was, used to compare heating or cooling energy across periods with different weather.
Half-hourly (HH) data
The electricity a meter records in each half hour: 48 readings a day, 17,520 in a normal year.
Heatmap
One chart of every half hour in a period, a column per day and a row per half hour, coloured by demand. Also called a carpet plot. More.
kVA
Kilovolt-ampere, the unit of apparent power that supply capacity is set in. kVA = kW ÷ power factor.
kW
Kilowatt, the rate electricity is being used. A half-hourly reading in kWh divided by 0.5 gives the average kW for that half hour.
kWh
Kilowatt-hour, the unit of energy you are billed for. 1 kW for one hour is 1 kWh.
Load factor
Average demand divided by peak demand. Hospitals have high load factors because demand never falls far.
Low-activity window
The hours the analyser treats as a 24-hour site's quietest, 01:00 to 05:00 by default, in place of closed hours.
MPAN
Meter point administration number: the 13-digit number on the bottom line of the supply number on an electricity bill. More.
Peak demand
The highest half-hourly average demand in a period, in kW or kVA. On many hospitals it falls on a hot summer afternoon.
Power factor
The ratio of real power (kW) to apparent power (kVA), between 0 and 1. Motors and fans lower it.

On the electricity bill

Capacity Market (CM)
A scheme paying providers to keep reliable capacity available, charged on use between 4pm and 7pm on working days from November to February. More.
Climate Change Levy (CCL)
A tax per kWh on energy used by businesses and public bodies. More.
Distribution Use of System (DUoS)
Charges for using the local network, set by the DNO: unit rates by time band, capacity charges and daily charges. More.
Market-wide half-hourly settlement (MHHS)
The move to settle every electricity meter in Great Britain on half-hourly data. More.
Non-energy costs
Everything in the electricity price apart from the wholesale energy: network charges, policy costs and taxes. More.
Profile class
The first two digits of the top line of the supply number. 00 means the supply is settled half-hourly. More.
Transmission Network Use of System (TNUoS)
Charges for the national high-voltage network, mainly a fixed daily charge per site for business supplies. More.
