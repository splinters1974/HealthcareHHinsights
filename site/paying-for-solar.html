Paying for solar on healthcare sites

Capital budgets, grants, power purchase agreements and leases each suit different organisations. For the NHS the questions are about capital limits and accounting as much as returns.

By Paul Johnston, Managing Director, Kinetic Energy Consulting · Last updated 22 November 2026

NHS organisations usually pay for solar from their capital allocation, with grant funding where it's available, or through a power purchase agreement (PPA) where a funder owns the system and the trust buys the electricity. Buying gives the most value over the system's life; a PPA needs no capital. Independent hospital groups have the same options plus tax relief on what they buy.

Grant funding for NHS solar

In March 2025, NHS England announced £100 million from Great British Energy for NHS trusts to install solar panels and battery storage.1 Great British Energy's Local Power Plan, published in February 2026, set out further support for local and public sector energy, including solar and batteries on public buildings.2 The Public Sector Decarbonisation Scheme, long the main grant for NHS heat and energy projects, isn't being funded for new rounds beyond 2027/28.3 Scotland and Wales run their own routes. Rounds open and close quickly, so check the current position on our funding page.

Power purchase agreements

Under an on-site PPA, a funder designs, installs, owns, insures and maintains the system, and you pay for the electricity you use at an agreed rate per kWh, usually below your grid price. Terms typically run 10 to 25 years, with the rate rising each year by a fixed percentage or with inflation. Before signing, check:

  • The rate and the escalator. A low starting rate rising steeply can end up dearer than the grid.
  • The accounting. Ask your finance team early whether the PPA would be treated as a lease, because that can bring it onto the balance sheet and against capital limits.
  • Roof works and access. What happens if a roof must be repaired, or a block demolished or rebuilt, during the term.
  • The end of the term. Transfer to the trust, extension or removal.
  • PFI and landlords. On a PFI building or a leased health centre, the project company or landlord must agree, and the term should fit their contract.

Measuring the share used on site from half-hourly data matters for a PPA too: you only pay for what you use, so the funder's offer depends on it.

Tax, VAT and business rates

  • Business rates. In England, on-site renewable generation, and batteries used with it, is exempt from business rates from April 2022.4
  • Capital allowances. Only relevant to organisations paying corporation tax, such as independent hospital groups. Solar is special rate plant, so the relief is through the 50% first-year allowance for companies or the Annual Investment Allowance, not full expensing.5,6
  • VAT. Recovery differs between NHS bodies and private companies. Confirm the position before comparing buying with a PPA.

General information only, not tax or accounting advice. Rules change; check with your finance team or adviser.

Which route suits you?

  • NHS trust with capital and a grant round open: buy, and use the grant to close the gap.
  • NHS trust with capital tied up in backlog: compare a PPA through an approved procurement route.
  • Independent hospital group: compare buying with capital allowances against a PPA.

Questions people ask

Is there grant funding for solar on NHS sites?

There has been: in March 2025 NHS England announced £100 million from Great British Energy for NHS trust solar and batteries. Funding rounds change often, so check the current position before planning around a grant.

Does a PPA count against an NHS trust's capital limit?

It can, if it is treated as a lease for accounting purposes. Ask your finance team to review the contract terms before you sign.

Can NHS trusts claim capital allowances on solar?

No, because NHS bodies do not pay corporation tax. Private hospital companies usually can, through the 50% first-year allowance or the Annual Investment Allowance.

Will solar raise our business rates?

Not in England, where on-site renewable generation and associated batteries are exempt from business rates from April 2022.
